Two axes of complexity: dimensionality and resolution

Each social pole is a square judgment of the same situation. Dimensionality subdivides the square into independent cells — one cell is uni-dimensional, a grid is multi-dimensional. Resolution grades each cell from binary (black / white) to continuous (shades of gray). The two poles sit at the ends of a distance scale: the gap between them is their polarization, and raising either axis of complexity pulls them together.

Polarization vs. dimensionality — current configuration

A few strongly contested cells plus additional cells the poles increasingly share. With an averaged metric the distance can only hold or fall as the square is subdivided; a summed metric (Manhattan, Euclidean) never averages down.

The idea. Polarization — the distance between two poles — is largest in the low-complexity corner: one cell, judged black-or-white. Subdividing the square (dimensionality) spreads the judgment across cells the sides often share; grading each cell (resolution) softens the clash within a cell. Either axis of representational complexity reduces the distance, and both together reduce it most — the mechanism the proposal advances for herding and depolarization. Switching the distance metric shows how much the conclusion depends on normalizing for dimensionality.
