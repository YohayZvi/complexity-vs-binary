Two axes of complexity: dimensionality and resolution

Representational complexity is not the opposite of “binary” — it is composed of two separable axes: dimensionality (how many scales a judgment uses) and resolution (how finely each scale is graded, from binary to continuous). Binary and uni-dimensional is simply the minimal corner. Two poles are two vectors at the ends of a distance scale; the gap between them is the measured distance, and raising either axis pulls them together. One strongly contested axis plus additional axes the poles increasingly share. With an averaged metric the distance can only hold or fall as dimensions are added; with a summed metric (Manhattan, Euclidean) it never falls — the metric choice decides the story.

The idea. Under an averaged metric, the maximum-distance corner is uni-dimensional and binary — one axis, us-or-them. Making the scale continuous softens that axis; adding dimensions averages the one clash against shared ground. Both moves reduce the distance — the loss / gain of representational complexity the proposal treats as the engine of herding and polarization. Switching the metric shows how much that conclusion depends on normalizing for dimensionality.
